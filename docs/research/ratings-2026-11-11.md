# TX Six-Man Football ratings: definition, research and audit

Nov 11, 2026. Built from a read-only audit of the repo (every game 2021 to 2026, the model code, a walk-forward
backtest recomputed game by game) and outside research on published rating systems. Nothing was changed.

## Bottom line

1. **The ratings are good.** They pick 85% of winners on games they had not seen, against 78% for Elo and 74% for last
   season's ratings. The two published high-school systems that test the same way report 83%.
2. **Our 45-point-rule treatment is original.** No published system treats a mercy-rule game as "won by at least 45".
   It is the statistically right idea, but nobody else has a benchmark to compare against.
3. **Three real problems to fix:**
   - **Forfeits count as real games.** 158 games (33 this season) recorded 1-0, 2-0 or 0-0 go into the ratings,
     although the site says they don't. 20 teams' 2026 ratings are off by more than 10 points (Gholson, Fort Davis,
     Cotton Center, Coolidge).
   - **Win probabilities are too confident.** When the site says 99%, the favorite wins 98.6%; at 95-99%, it wins 93%.
     And 61% of games end at 45 points, while the model expects 40%.
   - **Ranks look more precise than they are.** A rating is uncertain by about ±10 points, but neighboring UIL ranks
     differ by about 1 point. Most ▲/▼ arrows are noise (under 3 points of change).
4. **Fans need more than one number:** a range for each team, a record-based ranking beside the predictive one,
   strength of schedule, best win and worst loss, and why a team moved.

## 1. What a rating is (definition)

**In plain words.** Each team gets one number per season. The model finds the numbers that best explain every final
margin of that season at once. The difference between two teams' numbers, plus a home-field edge, is the projected
margin. Win probability comes from how far that projection is from zero, given how much six-man games vary.

**The parts:**
- **Projected margin** = home rating minus away rating, plus home field (about 4.8 points in 2026; 2.2 to 4.8 by
  season). Playoff games are neutral sites.
- **Game-to-game variation (sigma):** 23.6 points in 2026. Six-man margins swing a lot.
- **45-point rule:** a game ending 45 or more ahead counts as "at least 45", not the scoreboard margin.
- **Few games:** each rating starts pulled toward 0 (the average team). The pull fades as games come in. Under 4 games
  the rating shows grayed.
- **One scale for all leagues.** UIL, TAPPS, TAIAO and TCAF are rated together through the games they play against
  each other. Ranks and arrows are then counted within each league.
- **Not used:** games against teams outside our alignments (out-of-state, 11-man, JV).
- **Arrows:** rank now versus a refit one week earlier.

## 2. How other systems do it (research)

| System | Uses margin? | Blowouts | Home field | Early season |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Massey | Yes | Diminishing returns (square root, or a capped probability) | One constant | Prior |
| Colley | No, wins only | Ignored | None | Built-in prior |
| KRACH / Bradley-Terry | No | Ignored | None | Unbeaten teams break it |
| Sagarin Predictor | Yes | Capped (value never published) | One number | Unpublished |
| Elo with margin (538 NFL) | Yes, log of margin | Damped for favorites | 65 Elo points | Carries a third back to average |
| ESPN FPI / SP+ | Yes, efficiency | Unpublished | Yes | Returning starters and last year |
| Massachusetts MIAA (HS) | Yes | Capped at 14 | Not stated | None |
| SBLive (HS) | Yes | Not stated | None | None (noisy early) |
| NebPreps (HS, 2026) | Not stated | Not stated | Not stated | Last year, pulled to the middle; 83% winners |
| **Ours** | **Yes** | **Censored at 45 (unique)** | **Fit each season** | **Pull to 0; last season not used on the site** |

What the research says fans use most: a predictive ranking and a separate record-based ranking (ESPN's FPI and
Strength of Record; Massey's Power and Rating), a concrete strength of schedule, quality wins, and a range of
uncertainty. No high-school site publishes rating ranges or explains movement, so both would set us apart.

## 3. The data (audit, 2021 to 2026)

- **6,788 games:** 970, 1,076, 1,090, 1,195, 1,230 and 1,227 a season.
- **About 60% end at the 45-point rule** (UIL 60%, TAPPS 53%, TAIAO 44%).
- **Forfeits:** 158 games scored 2-0 or less, not excluded (see problem 1).
- **Duplicates:** about 7 games appear twice (same teams and score, dates 1 to 63 days apart). Tiny, but real.
- **Ties:** 15, mostly 0-0 (likely no-contests).
- **Leagues mix enough.** About 260 games a year cross leagues. All teams connect in one network in 2024 to 2026. In
  2021 to 2023 a few small groups (for example Benavides, Bruni and Runge) never played anyone else, so their ratings
  float.
- **Missing opponents:** in 2021, 20% of game results were dropped because the opponent is not in today's alignments;
  in 2026 only 5%. So old seasons are thinner.
- **League levels in 2026:** UIL +1.6, TAPPS +5.4, TAIAO -11.0, TCAF -17.1 (UIL Division I +14.5, Division II -11.7).
  TCAF's place rests on just 4 games against UIL, all ending at 45.

**Where it is strongest and weakest (2022 to 2026, forfeits removed):**

| Playoffs | 80.4% | 16.8 |
| TAPPS playoffs | 75.6% | 19.0 |
| Teams in different leagues | 83.3% | 15.7 |
| September | 83.0% | |
| October | 86.6% | |

**Probabilities, said versus happened:**

| Site said the favorite wins | Favorite actually won |
|---|---|
| 55% | 59% |
| 85% | 80% |
| 93% | 90% |
| 97% | 93% |
| 99%+ (a third of all games) | 98.6% |

**45-point endings:** the model expects 40%; 61% happen. Even in games projected within 5 points, 17% end at 45; the
model says 2%. That is why the 45-point chances stay off the site.

**Ranks that look odd but are explained:**
- **Westbrook, 4-6, #19 UIL:** the toughest schedule in the state, close losses to top teams (100-102 to Water Valley).
  It is a power rating, not a record rating. A record-based view would answer this for fans.
- **Whiteface #1 above Borden County #2,** although Borden County beat Whiteface 75-69: a 0.5-point gap with ±14 of
  uncertainty. Today's single rank hides that they are tied.
- **Unbeaten teams are not held down:** Medina 9-0 is #12 and Gordon 8-0 #15, both on soft schedules.

**Stability:** after week one, rank order barely changes week to week (0.98+ correlation), and top-10 membership
moves by 0 to 4 teams a week. But 84 of the 127 arrows shown this week come from rating changes under 3 points.

**Note on the G3 gate:** the backtest that passes G3 uses last season's ratings as a starting point (rho 0.6). The site
does not, and it is a little worse (MAE 15.1 versus 14.0). Both beat both baselines.

## 5. What to fix, in order

1. **Stop rating forfeits.** One shared forfeit rule (the one the pages already use) applied to the ratings, the game
   counts and the backtest; 0-0 is a no-contest. Rerun every season.
2. **Calibrate win probability.** Fit the variation honestly on week-before predictions (it is closer to 26 points
   than 21), let it grow for mismatches, and show at most ">95%" or ">99%" only when it is earned. Fix the
   week-before projections to use the variation known that week.
3. **Start each season from last season.** Use last season's ratings, pulled 40% toward average, as the starting
   point, as the backtest already does. Biggest gain is in September.
4. **Start each team from its league's level,** not from zero: UIL Division I, Division II, TAPPS, TAIAO and TCAF
   averages fit from the games. Fixes isolated and few-game teams.
5. **Playoff home edge:** the listed home team still outperforms by 2.5 points at "neutral" sites; give it a partial
   edge.
6. **Data hygiene:** drop duplicate games, flag non-zero ties, one definition of "games rated", arrows on Monday to
   Sunday weeks.
7. **Keep unknown opponents in the fit** (hidden from tables), so 2021 to 2023 stop losing a fifth of their games.

## 6. Better rankings pages

1. **Rating range:** each team's rating with a ± band, and a rank range ("#3 to #13").
2. **Tiers instead of false precision:** group teams whose ratings overlap; arrows only when a rating really moved
   (more than 3 to 5 points); grayed teams show "NR", not a rank.
3. **Record view:** "how hard is this record against this schedule", from our own win probabilities, beside the
   predictive rating, so a 9-0 team on a soft schedule and a 4-6 team on the hardest one both make sense.
4. **Strength of schedule, best win and worst loss** on every table row and program page.
5. **Why it moved:** each week, the game that moved a team most against its projection.
6. **Scorecard on /rankings/how:** said-versus-happened table and plot, split by season, playoffs, and league.
